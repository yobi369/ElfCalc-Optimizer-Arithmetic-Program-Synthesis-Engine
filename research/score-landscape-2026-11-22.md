## A hard lower bound on the requested 6,000–8,000 range

There are **26,898 distinct target values that are not already present in their puzzle's starting registers**. Each such value must be written into a register by at least one arithmetic instruction. One instruction has one destination register and produces one value. Therefore:

**The total score cannot be lower than 26,898 instructions.**

This is a simple instance-derived lower bound; it does not rely on any third-party proof package. A separate public research repository reports a stronger combined puzzle-level lower bound of 37,987, but that certificate collection has not yet been independently rechecked in this project: https://github.com/Hansen0223/Santa_2026.

Consequently, 6,000–8,000 is mathematically unattainable under the stated scoring rules and this puzzle file. We should redirect that ambition toward a feasible frontier rather than spend time optimizing toward an impossible number.

## Research sequence

1. Acquire and hash the newest publicly available, properly licensed candidate/program pool. Preserve row-level provenance and author/license notices.
2. Rebuild a per-puzzle portfolio from all usable public programs, keeping the shortest independently replay-valid program for each puzzle.
3. Reproduce the current public frontier before attempting novel search. Compare score, hashes, source provenance, and row-level differences.
4. Use stratified, resumable experiments on the hardest puzzles. Prioritize independent target branches, graph/DAG rewriting, register-lifetime scheduling, and exact local search.
5. Accept only strict per-puzzle improvements that pass the independent VM and official CSV serialization checks.
6. Recompute the full 1,000-puzzle score and hash after every merge. Never infer a score improvement from partial runs.
